1. Who we are

Akwaaba is a booking service for pre-arranged airport pickups in Ghana. It connects travellers arriving at Kotoka International Airport, Accra (ACC), Prempeh I International Airport, Kumasi (KMS), and other airports we add, with independent drivers who have been checked and approved by us.

Akwaaba is operated by Astrovenza Ventures ("Akwaaba", "we", "us"), the data controller for the personal data described in this policy.

Drivers who use Akwaaba are independent operators. They are not our employees. When a traveller pays a driver, that payment is between the traveller and the driver. Akwaaba does not collect, hold or pass on fares.

2. Who this policy covers and which laws apply

This policy covers:

  • Travellers: anyone who books a pickup, and the lead passenger named on a booking.
  • Drivers: people who apply to drive with Akwaaba, and approved drivers.
  • Visitors to our website and web app.

Staff and contractors receive a separate internal notice.

We process personal data under the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) of Ghana, under the supervision of the Data Protection Commission (DPC).

Many of our travellers book from abroad. Where personal data originating from another country is sent to Ghana for processing, we process it in line with the data protection law of that country, as Act 843 s.18(2) requires. In practice, for travellers who book while in the United Kingdom or the European Economic Area, we apply the UK GDPR or the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) to their data. Those laws can also apply to us directly because we offer our service to people in those places (GDPR Art. 3(2)).

Criminal record information is special personal data under Act 843 s.37. We collect only the result of the check (for example "clear" or "not clear" and the date), we collect it only with your express consent, and we use it only to decide whether you can drive with Akwaaba. 4.3 Technical and security data (everyone)

When you use our website or web app, our hosting providers automatically record technical data: your IP address, browser type, the pages and functions you used, and the date and time. We use this to keep the service running, to protect it against attacks and abuse, and to investigate problems. Drivers and staff who sign in also generate sign-in records (time, IP address, device type).

Drivers who sign in keep a sign-in session in their browser's storage. We do not use advertising cookies or third-party analytics. See section 14.

4.4 What we do not collect

  • Card numbers, mobile money PINs, payment tokens. Drivers pay their subscription on Paystack's secure checkout. Paystack, a payment service provider licensed by the Bank of Ghana, handles the payment details. We receive only a payment reference, the amount, the date and the type of payment method (for example "mobile money").
  • Fares. Travellers pay drivers directly. We do not process fare payments.
  • Live location. Akwaaba does not track the location of travellers or drivers.
  • Data about children. We do not ask for the names or details of children travelling. We only ask how many passengers there are and whether a child seat is needed. Bookings must be made by someone aged 18 or over.

Automated decisions. Our system assigns bookings automatically. It picks a driver who is approved, has an active subscription, drives the right vehicle size and is free at the landing time. It also blocks a booking when the same phone number already has a pickup at another airport within six hours, and it stops assigning pickups to a driver whose subscription has lapsed. If you think one of these automated decisions has treated you unfairly, you can ask for a person to review it (Act 843 s.41; GDPR Art. 22).

7. Keeping your data secure

We use technical and organisational measures that suit the risks involved (Act 843 s.28; GDPR Art. 32). They include:

  • encrypted connections (HTTPS) everywhere;
  • encryption of stored data by our hosting provider, and additional encryption of travellers' names, phone numbers, emails and addresses inside our database;
  • row-level access rules in our database, so drivers can see only their own trips;
  • staff access only with a strong password and a second sign-in step (authenticator app), with automatic sign-out and an audit trail of staff actions;
  • prices and payment confirmations checked on our servers, never trusted from a browser;
  • no card or mobile money details held by us; and
  • written contracts requiring the same standards from our service providers.

No system is perfectly secure. If something goes wrong, we will act as described in section 15.

8. International transfers and where your data is stored

Our database and servers are run by Supabase and Vercel, which store and process data in London, United Kingdom. Their support teams and some of their own suppliers may access data from the United States or other countries. So your data is stored and processed outside Ghana.

  • Under Act 843: Act 843 does not set special rules for sending data abroad. We disclose where your data goes in this policy and in our DPC registration, and our providers are bound by written contracts to protect it (s.30).
  • Under the GDPR and UK GDPR: where your data is subject to these laws and goes to a country without an adequacy decision, including Ghana and the United States, we rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, or the provider's certification under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, as applicable. You can ask us for a copy of the safeguards.
  • Foreign data subjects (Act 843 s.18(2)): data you send from abroad, for example while booking from London, is processed in line with your home country's data protection law, as section 2 explains.

12. Marketing

We send marketing messages only if you have given us your prior written consent, by ticking an unticked box (Act 843 s.40). Booking a pickup does not sign you up to marketing. Every marketing message tells you how to stop receiving them, and you can also email us to stop. Booking confirmations and messages about your trip are not marketing and are sent whatever your marketing choice.

13. Children

Akwaaba is for adults. Bookings must be made by someone aged 18 or over, and the booking form asks you to confirm this. We do not knowingly collect personal data about children. We ask only for the number of passengers and whether a child seat is needed. If you believe a child has given us personal data, contact us and we will delete it.

14. Cookies and browser storage

  • Travellers: the booking page does not set cookies.
  • Drivers: the driver app keeps your sign-in session in your browser's local storage so you stay signed in. This is strictly necessary for the service.
  • Fonts and code libraries: our pages load their fonts and code from our own servers, not from third parties. The only outside links are the WhatsApp and phone links you choose to tap.
  • We do not use advertising cookies, tracking pixels or third-party analytics. If we add analytics, we will update this policy and ask for consent where the law requires it.

15. If there is a data breach

If personal data is accessed or acquired by someone without authority:

  1. We act immediately to contain the breach and protect the data.
  2. We notify the Data Protection Commission and the people affected as soon as reasonably practicable after we discover the breach (Act 843 s.31). Our own target is within 72 hours.
  3. Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, we notify the relevant supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach, unless it is unlikely to put anyone at risk (Art. 33). We tell affected people without undue delay where the risk to them is high (Art. 34).
  4. We report cyber incidents to Ghana's Cyber Security Authority (CERT-GH).
  5. Our notice tells you what happened, what data was involved, what we are doing, what you can do to protect yourself, and who to contact. Where we know who obtained the data, we tell you that too.

We send notices by SMS, email, a notice on our website, or the media, depending on how many people are affected. We delay telling affected people only if the DPC or a security agency asks us to, so as not to hinder a criminal investigation.
